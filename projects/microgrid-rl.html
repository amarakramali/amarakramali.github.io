From MP1 to MP2

The hardware becomes a control problem.

MP1 builds the physical energy node. MP2 turns that setup into a physics-based digital twin and asks a sharper question: can learned source controllers outperform droop and remain useful when an MPC forecast is wrong?

Bus voltage is not clamped by an ideal supply. It moves with the net source current through the 7.5 F buffer, so voltage becomes the visible consequence of every control decision — the DC analogue of frequency in a larger AC grid.

Plant model

A small grid with real constraints.

The simulation integrates bus physics at one-second substeps. Only the decision interval changes — 1 s, 5 s or 30 s — so the study isolates controller speed instead of changing the underlying plant. Each 48-hour slow/default episode combines renewable availability, two load profiles and a dispatchable source with startup constraints.

Learning setup

One policy per source, one shared objective.

The headline controller is best described as Multi-Policy PPO: four PPO policies receive the same 21-dimensional global observation and optimise one cooperative reward. It is not the original centralised-critic MAPPO architecture, so the project names the implementation precisely.

Behaviour cloning from droop provides the warm-start. The Phase 6 matrix then compares it with local-observation IPPO, single-agent PPO, constrained Lagrangian PPO, domain-randomised training and two inference-time shield settings. Classical references include droop, heuristic MPC with three forecast-noise levels and convex MPC.

Why the gate structure mattered. Single-agent PPO looked strongest during training, then failed to generalise: in the default held-out evaluation its critical supply collapsed to 30.8%. Phase 6 reports the test result, not the attractive training curve.

Experiment scale

The result took 157 run-hours — not one lucky seed.

The main Phase 6 campaign trained five controller families at three action intervals and five independent seeds: 75 complete jobs, 200 iterations each. Their measured wall-clock spans sum to 156.8 hours; the matrix itself ran from 14 to 25 June 2026.

A headline Multi-Policy PPO run took a median 2.41 hours. Single-agent PPO finished in only 0.67 hours because it learns one shared policy instead of four — but its held-out default evaluation collapsed. Faster training did not mean better generalisation.

Phase 6 results

Droop is stable — but it drains the battery and sheds the critical load.

In the completed slow configuration, headline Multi-Policy PPO reduces mean voltage RMSE from 0.554 to 0.291, raises critical-load supply from 65.6% to 98.9%, and ends with mean battery SOC 0.786 instead of 0.052. These are held-out Phase 6 results, not numbers carried forward from an earlier phase.

Forecast robustness

MPC degrades exactly where the learned policy is independent.

With a perfect forecast, MPC is competitive. At the Phase 6 15% noise setting, its RMSE rises to 0.548 and critical supply falls to 68.4%. The learned controller has no forecast input, so this error channel does not exist for it.

What Phase 6 established

Strong evidence, with the caveats left visible.

EstablishedCompleted gates
  • All 75 planned training runs completed and passed health checks.
  • The held-out slow and default evaluations use seeds 100–119, disjoint from training and tuning.
  • Multi-Policy PPO clearly improves over slow droop on voltage, critical supply and battery state.
  • Mild domain randomisation is neutral to beneficial in the completed configurations.
  • A 27-setting reward-weight sweep confirms robustness but also a real RMSE trade-off.
In progressFinal study closure
  • Fast-configuration MARL evaluation is not yet part of the published headline.
  • Storm, overcast and load-spike stress runs still need the final statistical synthesis.
  • Confidence intervals, paired tests and Holm correction belong to the final analysis gate.
  • Inference-time shields do not dominate the base controller; their safety–performance trade-off remains explicit.
  • Transfer to the physical MP1 node is planned work, not a completed result.
